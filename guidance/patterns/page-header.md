---
title: 'Page header'
description: 'Choosing the right header treatment for each content type on digital.gov.au.'
---

The page header establishes the page title, gives users a brief orientation, and signals the content type. The right treatment depends on the type of page you are building.

Always match the header title to the H1. Do not write an alternative title for the header. Keep descriptions under 25–30 words. Do not use decorative images on reference content.

## Variants by content type

**Standard content – guides, resources, policies**

Use a simple title and short description header with no image or colour treatment. This is the default. Most pages on digital.gov.au use this variant.

**Program or campaign pages**

Use a title, description, and optional image or approved colour band. Only include an image if it is directly relevant to the program or campaign – do not use generic stock photography. The image must have a text alternative.

**Report landing pages**

Use a title, description, publication date, and a PDF download link in the header. Do not place the download link only in the body – users arriving via search often look for the download before reading the page. Do not include a decorative image.

**Section landing pages**

Use the full-width [Banner](/components/banner/) component with the section title and a two to three sentence description. An optional background image or approved colour treatment may be applied, consistent with design system and brand guidelines. Keep the description free of jargon.

**Focus mode pages (multi-step forms)**

Use the minimal header variant: title only, no main navigation, no image or colour treatment. See the [focus mode pattern](/patterns/focus-mode/) for full guidance on this layout.

## Rules

- The header title must match the H1 of the page and the label used in any navigation that links to it.
- Descriptions must be under 25–30 words.
- Do not use decorative images on reference content – guides, policies, and resource pages do not need imagery.
- Do not add a full-width hero to a page that functions as a reference document rather than a destination.
