A communique is a formal, dated record of a committee's decisions and discussions. Unlike a news article or summary, it is an official published account of one meeting. It records what the committee discussed and decided, for agencies, ministers, and the public.

Bodies such as the Secretaries Digital and Data Committee publish communiques on digital.gov.au. They provide transparency around governance decisions, and readers cite them as formal records over time.

## When to use

Use a communique when:
- a formal committee or body has met and has decisions or outcomes to publish
- the record must stay publicly available and carry the name of the committee as a whole
- the content must survive as an accurate and authoritative account of proceedings

Do not use a communique for informal updates, program news, or internal meeting notes. Those formats are better suited to the News / updates page variant described below, or to a resource or guide page.

## Structure

A communique follows this standard structure:

1. **Date and committee name** – state the full name of the committee and the date of the meeting in the page title or opening line
2. **Attendees** (optional) – list members present, by role rather than name where appropriate
3. **Items discussed** – a structured account of topics considered at the meeting
4. **Decisions made** – clearly stated outcomes, attributed to the committee as a body
5. **Next steps or follow-up actions** – any agreed actions, timelines, or responsibilities arising from the meeting

Keep sections concise. A communique records outcomes rather than transcribing the meeting. Omit deliberative detail that is not relevant to the published decision.

## Writing conventions

Follow the [Australian Government Style Manual](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au) throughout. Key conventions for communiques include:

- Use past tense throughout: 'the committee discussed', 'members agreed', 'the chair noted'
- State decisions in active voice: write `the committee agreed to publish the framework by July`, not `it was agreed that the framework would be published`
- Do not attribute statements to individual members unless they are formally minuted
- Use the full committee name on first reference; abbreviate on subsequent references only if the abbreviation is widely understood
- Avoid editorialising – record the decisions, not commentary on their quality or significance

## Web UI components

Use the following UI components when building communique pages in the CMS.

**Page header (hero)** – Use a simple title-and-description header. The title should follow the pattern: [Committee name] – [date] (for example, 'Secretaries Digital and Data Committee – 13 March 2025'). Keep the description under 25 words.

**Rich text body** – The main content area. Use H2 headings for each structural section (Attendees, Items discussed, Decisions, Next steps). Use numbered lists for decisions and action items.

**Related content cards** – Add two to four cards at the bottom of the page. Link to previous communiques from the same committee, and to policy pages the communique references. Standard-length communiques need no in-page navigation.

## Sub-page variants

Communiques do not typically require sub-pages. However, the following variant addresses lower-formality publishing needs for programs and initiatives.

**News / updates page** – a lower-formality update for program or initiative news (for example, Observatory monthly updates). CMS-managed; it does not need the same structural rigour as a communique. Write it as short prose of 1–3 paragraphs, date-stamped. It needs no related content cards. Use this variant to keep stakeholders informed rather than to publish a formal committee record.
